Awards
All iGEM awards recognize and celebrate different forms of accomplishment, excellence, and contribution across the Competition. Together, they reflect what iGEM values and help highlight the many ways teams can demonstrate meaningful work, growth, and impact.
[IMG] https://static.igem.org/websites/competition/2025/judging/special-prizes.webp
Medals allow us to celebrate the accomplishments of iGEM teams in using synthetic biology to address global challenges. Through the Medal system, we recognize the core principles of iGEM: engineering, respect, community, and honesty.
There is no limit to the number of Medals awarded each year. All teams compete only with themselves by meeting defined Medal Criteria, and all teams are eligible to earn a Medal.
## Medals
[IMG] Bronze medal
Bronze
Awarded to teams that have participated in iGEM, presented their work, and made meaningful contributions that future teams can build on.
[IMG] Silver medal
Silver
Awarded to teams that have addressed the key pillars of iGEM projects: Engineering Success and Human Practices.
[IMG] Gold medal
Gold
Awarded to teams that have shown excellence, beyond what is required for Silver, across General Bio Engineering and their chosen Specializations.
## Scoring Medals
Every medal criterion is scored the same way:
- Individual judges evaluate the work documented on the Standard URL on team wikis (and on the relevant Registry entries, where applicable).
- For each criterion, every judge records a yes or no for the team. The yes votes are counted, and the criterion's final result is yes when a majority of judges vote yes.
- Ties are ruled in favor of the team (for example, three yes votes and three no votes means the criterion is met).
- A team earns the highest medal for which every criterion (at that level and at all levels below it) has a final result of yes.
The only difference between the levels is how each judge's yes/no for a criterion is produced:
- Bronze and Silver: each judge records their yes/no directly for each criterion.
- Gold (new in 2026): the criterion is assessed through the three Special Awards the team selected on the Judging Form. Judges do not cast a separate Gold vote. Each judge already scores those awards on their rubrics, and that judge's yes/no for an award is inferred from their score: yes if the score meets the performance standard, no otherwise. These votes are then counted in exactly the same way as above. Each of the three selected awards must reach a final result of yes for the Gold criterion to be met.
## Judging Criteria
All Medal criteria are listed below with a brief description and their Standard URLs. For your own pages, replace "example" with your team wiki page.
### Bronze — all criteria must be met
1Competition Deliverables
Complete the following Competition Deliverables: Safety Forms, Wiki, Judging Form, Presentation Video, and Judging Session.
Guidance
- Safety Forms
- Wiki
- Judging Form
- Presentation Video
- Judging Session
2Project Attributions
Provide a detailed description of what your team members did and what others did for every aspect of your project, using the standardized Attributions Form.
Guidance
See the Attributions Form page for guidelines. It must be filled out completely, accurately, and honestly. We recommend starting early and updating it often.
3Contribution
Make a useful contribution for future iGEM teams.
Required Standard URL: 2026.igem.wiki/example/contribution ↗
Guidance
Teams must document their contribution and explain why it is a contribution to fellow iGEMers. There is no single definition: be creative and generous with the knowledge, data, or tools you provide.
Examples:
- Add new substantial documentation to an existing Part on the Registry.
- Build on existing software or hardware.
- Document troubleshooting useful to future teams.
### Silver — all Bronze and Silver criteria must be met
1Engineering Success
Demonstrate engineering success in a technical aspect of your project by going through at least one iteration of the engineering design cycle.
Required Standard URL: 2026.igem.wiki/example/engineering ↗
Guidance
Engineering success can be achieved by documenting your effort to follow the engineering design cycle: Design → Build → Test → Learn
- We invite you to think about ways to tackle and solve one or more of your project's problems and use synthetic biology tools and/or experimental techniques to generate expected results.
- When you have completed the cycle once, think about and document what changes in design you would make for the next iteration(s) of the cycle.
- For example, you can design and build a new Part, measure its performance, document whether it worked or not, and propose how the results could inform the next design or steps (documentation must be on the Registry).
See the Engineering pages for guidance.
2Human Practices
Explain how you have determined that your work is responsible and good for the world.
Required Standard URL: 2026.igem.wiki/example/human-practices ↗
Guidance
Some questions to help guide you:
- What values— environmental, social, moral, scientific, or other—did you have in mind when designing your project?
- Which resources or communities did you consult to ensure those values are appropriate in the context of your project?
- What evidence do you have to show that your project is responsible and good for the world?
- What impact will your project have?
- Who are your proposed end users?
- How do you envision others using your project?
- How would you implement your project in the real world?
Note: You should draw on personal reflections, background research, and/or engagement with communities relevant to your project as you think about the above.
See the Human Practices page for more.
### Gold — all Bronze, Silver and Gold criteria must be met
1Excellence in Synthetic Biology
Demonstrate excellence in your work toward three Special Awards of your choice. At least one must come from the General Bio Engineering category and at least one from the Specializations category.
Note: Software & AI Village teams cannot select Best Software Award as a Gold Medal criterion.
Guidance
General Bio Engineering
- Best Measurement
- Best Model
- Best New Basic Part
- Best New Composite Part
- Best New Improved Part
- Best Part Collection
Specializations
- Best Education
- Best Entrepreneurship
- Best Hardware
- Inclusivity Award
- Best Integrated Human Practices
- Best Alternative Platform
- Safety and Security Award
- Best Software
- Best Sustainable Development Impact
The third selection may come from either list. Teams may select fewer than three Special Awards, but will not qualify for a Gold Medal. You select your awards on the Judging Form.
