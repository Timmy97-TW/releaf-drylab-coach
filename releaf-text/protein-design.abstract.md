## Abstract
This section is the dry lab work that chose ReLEAF’s three protectants and decided what to change about each one before any DNA was ordered.
The first thing it established was that there was little to gain by redesigning the binding sites themselves, which evolution had already brought close to as good as they get. The engineering moved instead to the construct and the delivery, and those decisions came from the pipelines around us rather than from this one: how short a molecule can be made before the Bioreactor struggles to produce it, what conditions the Hydroponics System actually delivers it into, what dose Math Modelling says has to arrive, and how a finished sequence becomes DNA a bacterium will read, which is Codon Optimization, a pipeline that split off from this one once designing a protein and getting a cell to secrete it turned out to be separate problems.
It is also a record of what we got wrong and caught in time. A purification tag that every earlier check had approved turned out, once simulated, to disable the exact contact the peptide depends on, which would have given the wet lab a molecule that purifies beautifully and cannot work. For the second protectant we mapped one half of an interaction with confidence and then showed that the other half cannot be predicted from what is currently known, so that design goes to the bench as one testable mutation rather than as a model we could not defend. The third is an enzyme whose largest available gain is not a mutation at all, but a change to the medium it works in.
