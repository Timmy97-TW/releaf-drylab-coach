HOW TO WRITE A NOTE
- anchor: copy exact text from ONE line of the text file (4 to 30 words). Check it occurs
  exactly once in that file with a Python substring count before saving; if it repeats,
  lengthen it. Never anchor in the navigation at the top of the file, in the footer, or in
  an [IMG] alt line. [CAPTION] lines are fine and figure captions are worth reviewing.
  Anchor the phrase the note is about; for a paragraph-level note, anchor its first clause.
- praise: name the exact move and why a judge rewards it ("this sentence gives the number,
  the n it rests on and the thing it cannot show, in one line"). Link a winner excerpt doing
  the same thing when one exists, so the students see they are already at winner level. No
  generic "great job", and no praise for a sentence you are also asking to change.
- restructure / cut / clarify: say what is wrong in one sentence, then give "after": one way
  to write it. The "after" keeps the students' facts and voice, invents nothing (use
  [number], [date], [name] placeholders where a fact is missing), and reads like a careful
  student wrote it. Keep it shorter than the original whenever you can.
- mistake: typos, grammar that changes meaning, wrong or inconsistent numbers, units, names
  or terms, and claims that contradict another ReLEAF page. Quote both sides. Check numbers
  against the other pages in releaf-text/ before calling something a mistake.
- clarify: jargon, an acronym used before it is defined, a tool or method name a judge will
  not know, a missing "so what". Our judges read many pages a day and are not in our field.
- suggest: what the ballot questions ask for that the page does not give, tied to the spot
  where it should go, with the winner excerpt that shows how. Never ask for work that was
  not done: ask for the sentence that reports what was done.
- excerpts: up to 2 ids per note, only when the winner passage really shows the move. Quote
  ids exactly as they appear in the bank.
- title: 8 plain words or fewer, sentence case, no numbers in it. body: 1 to 4 short
  sentences, second person, direct and kind.

HOUSE STYLE for everything you write: plain, specific, short sentences. No em dashes or en
dashes as punctuation. Do not use "rather than" or "not X but Y" contrasts. Do not use the
words delve, robust, leverage, crucial, seamless, pivotal, showcase, underscore, foster,
holistic. No hype. English only. The students are undergraduates, several writing in their
second language: be exact about the problem and never condescending.
